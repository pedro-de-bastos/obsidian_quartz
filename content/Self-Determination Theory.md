Ryan and Deci identified three sub-components that explain three innate psychological 'needs' that predict human growth and motivation (Ryan and Deci, Self-Determination Theory and the Facilitation of Intrinsic Motivation, Social Development, and Well-Being.):
- Competence
- Autonomy
- Relatedness

## Competence
"Accordingly, optimal challenges, effectance-prorooting feedback, and freedom from demeaning evaluations were all found to facilitate [[Intrinsic Motivation]]" (Ryan and Deci)

## Autonomy
"that feelings of competence will not enhance intrinsic motivation unless accompanied by a sense of autonomy or, in attributional terms, by an internal [[Locus of Causality]]" (Ryan and Deci)

## Relatedness
Ryan and Deci (p71) discuss evidence of how dismissal, or lack of enthusiasm, or even a cold demeanor by one's parents or teachers can lead to a decrease in intrinsic motivation. They recognize that a lack of 'relatedness' can undermine int. motivation, but that relatedness is not always necessary since many 'solo' activities are intrinsically motivated.

When reading about relatedness, I wonder if the undermining effect of a parent or teacher's dismissal isn't actually an attack towards one's sense of *value* of a particular activity. SDT certainly connects to [[Control-Value Theory]], where motivation is directly impacted by one's sense of 'value'. If one's parents or teachers respond apathetically to, say, an excited account of what one is doing or trying to accomplish, perhaps it is the re-assessment of the activity's 'value' that undermines motivation.
