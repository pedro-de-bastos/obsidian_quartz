### **1. Replicative Knowing**

  

**Being able to recall or reproduce information that was explicitly taught.**

- Memorized facts, definitions, procedures
    
- What traditional tests measure
    
- Quickly forgotten over time

- In the [[Knowledge-Change Processes]] described by Chi and Ruth (2014), this would map onto the 'Store' knowledge, and in the [[ICAP Framework]], this would map onto the 'passive' mode of engagement.
    

---

### **2. Applicative Knowing**

  

**Being able to apply previously learned knowledge or skills to solve a new problem.**

- Classic “transfer out”
    
- Using learned procedures in new contexts
    
- Often harder than expected; many studies show failure here
- In the [[Knowledge-Change Processes]], this would map onto the 'integrate' knowledge-change process, and onto the 'active' mode of engagement.
    

---

### **3. Interpretive Knowing**

  

**Using prior experiences, concepts, and perspectives to make sense of new situations.**

- What you _notice_, the questions you ask, the assumptions you bring
    
- Shapes how you learn in unfamiliar contexts
    
- Often implicit and not easily assessed by tests
    
- The foundation of “transferring in” and adaptive expertise (see [[Transferring in vs. Transferring Out]])
- This would map onto the 'infer' knowledge-change process, and onto the 'constructive' mode of engagement. If we observe interpretive knowing in an inter-personal or human-ai case, we might also say this is a co-infer knowledge process, and an interactive engagement mode in ICAP.
