A few examples of [[CSCL (Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning)]] scripts:

**1. MURDER Script**

- **Concept:** This script focuses on text comprehension and uses a "reciprocal schema" where partners switch duties. The name is an acronym for the phases: **M**ood, **U**nderstanding, **R**ecall, **D**etection, **E**laboration, and **R**eview.
    
- **Process:** Two students work together on a text. After setting the mood, both read a passage. One student (the Summarizer) recalls the text aloud, while the other (the Listener) detects errors or omissions. Both partners then elaborate on the content before switching roles for the next text passage. Finally, they review the material together.
    

**2. Universanté Script**

- **Concept:** This is a "Jigsaw schema" script designed to facilitate learning through social interdependence. It requires students from different nations to work together on problem cases.
    
- **Process:**
    
    1. **Mixed National Groups:** Students first read and discuss a case in teams composed of different nationalities.
        
    2. **National Groups:** Students then regroup with peers of their own nationality to create a "national fact sheet" based on the cases they discussed.
        
    3. **Thematic Groups:** Students regroup again (mixed nationalities) to compare and compile the fact sheets.
        
    4. **Return:** Finally, they return to their original group to solve the initial case using the shared knowledge.
        

**3. ArgueGraph Script**

- **Concept:** This script uses a "conflict schema" to trigger argumentation. It relies on measuring differences in opinion to pair students who disagree.
    
- **Process:** Students first answer a questionnaire individually. Their answers are plotted on a graph, and the teacher pairs students who have highly conflicting opinions (large distance on the graph). These pairs must then discuss their conflicting views and fill out the questionnaire again together. Finally, each student writes a synthesis of the arguments for a specific item.
    

**4. Social Script**

- **Concept:** This script focuses on the roles of "Analyst" and "Critic" to improve case analysis skills. It uses scaffolding (sentence starters) to help students learn how to critique effectively.
    
- **Process:** Students work in groups of three to analyze case studies. For any given case, one student acts as the **Analyst** (writing the analysis), while the other two act as **Critics** (reviewing the analysis). The critics are given specific prompts (e.g., "These aspects are not clear to me yet") to guide their feedback. The analyst then revises their work based on this critique.
