This was an example used by Schwartz et al (2005) to illustrate a key difference between how [[Sequestered Problem Solving]] and [[Preparation For Learning (PFL)]] assessments differ in how they assess the 'intelligence' of particular students.

In this experiment, Kay Burgess had 5th graders and college students create a plan for how to increase the bald eagle population in their state.

In the 'SPS' portion of the experiment, researchers collected the answers from 5th graders and college students. They found that, while the answers provided by college students were better written and considerably more structured, the answers were 'as far' from an expert answer as 5th grader's answers.

In the second portion of the experiment, the 5th graders and college students were asked to note down what questions they would ask that would help guide their problem solving. Now, 5th graders developed questions that were simpler (like "what do eagles eat") and less insightful than what what college students were developing.

In short, the college student's preparedness for future learning was much higher, which is omitted from 'SPS' tasks.
