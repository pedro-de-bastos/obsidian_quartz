**Sequestered Problem Solving** is a traditional way of assessing learning where a student is:

- **isolated (sequestered)** from outside resources
    
- **given a fixed problem**
    
- **expected to solve it using only what they’ve already learned**
    
- **with no access to tools, hints, or new information**
    

In other words:

> **It measures what a student already knows and can apply directly, without any support or opportunity to learn during the task.**

This form of assessment implicitly assumes that:

- learning = acquiring stable knowledge
    
- performance = a good proxy for learning
    
- transfer = applying learned procedures to similar problems

It is the dominant model behind most standardized tests and many classroom quizzes.

SPS stands in CONTRAST to [[Preparation For Learning (PFL)]], proposed by Schwartz et al (2005).

Shwarts et al (2005) argue that SPS rewards memorization, application of known problem-solving steps and procedures. It does not measure one's ability to learn new procedures, optimize existing ones, make use of new tools and resources to problem-solve.
