Strongly related to the idea of 'autonomy' in [[Self-Determination Theory]], the perceived locus of causality describes whether one attributes a behavior to an internal drive, or to an external pressure or incentive.

Ryan and Deci describe how factors like "threats, deadlines, directives, pressured evaluations, and imposed goals diminish intrinsic motivation because, like tangible rewards, they conduce toward an external perceived locus of causality" (Ryan and Deci, p70)

"Students taught with a more controlling approach not only lose initiative but learn less effectively, especially when learning requires conceptual, creative processing" (Ryan and Deci, p71)

## Internalization and Integration

Even extrinsically-motivated behaviors can become more self-determined through 'internalization and integration'. Internalization means adopting values or regulations proposed by someone else, and integration is further transformation of that value/regulation so that it 'emanates from one's sense of self' (Ryan and Deci, p71).
