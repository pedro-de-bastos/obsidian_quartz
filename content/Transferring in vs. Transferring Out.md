These are key terms in [[Preparation For Learning (PFL)]]. In fact, the idea of 'transferring in' is the central phenomenon that explains what preparation for learning is: the idea that we can bring in mental models, habits of mind, foundational concepts, etc, to new situations, and use that baggage as a scaffold for learning (for coming up with hypotheses, questions, ideas, etc).
# **Transferring Out**
  
**The direct application of previously learned knowledge, procedures, or strategies to a new problem.**

It answers the question: _“Can you use what you already learned to solve this?”_

An example of transferring out would be to apply a known formula, like $F=MA$ , to a physics question asking you to find the force given mass and acceleration. 

---

# **Transferring In**


**The use of prior experiences, intuitions, analogies, and interpretive frameworks to understand and learn within a new situation.**

It answers the question: _“How does what you already know help you learn here?”_

This is more akin to what we know from Minerva e.g. transfer/far-transfer. When students used ideas about wolf repopulation, or even Lotka-Volterra equations, in their attempt to solve [[Burgess’ Original Eagle Challenge]], they were transferring in.
